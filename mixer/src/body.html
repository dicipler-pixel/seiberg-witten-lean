Simulations

Twelve eyes in the order of the paper. Every eye is driven by the feed above: a vacuum u = ⟨Tr φ²⟩, the radii of the closed paths, a state with magnetic and electric charges (n_m, n_e), and the mass m that breaks N = 2 to N = 1. In each readout the computed column is obtained from the two integrals of Section 6 and the paper column is the paper’s own formula or value; green means the two agree. The normalization is the paper’s: the singular points are at u = 1, u = −1 and infinity. The Lean chip records that the library does not yet contain the statement.

Record

The state of the Lean library, what this page adds that the paper does not print, and what it leaves out.

The Lean library today

  • Repository dicipler-pixel/seiberg-witten-lean at commit 777ea49 (10 November 2026): the source reference and the build configuration. It contains no theorem yet.
  • The check “Lean proof check” passed on that commit. It builds every module against Mathlib and fails on sorry, admit, native_decide or a new axiom.
  • Every panel on this page therefore carries the chip “Lean: not yet formalized”. The numbers here are recomputations, not proofs.

Asymptotic statements

  • a ≈ √(2u) and a_D ≈ i √(2u) ln u/π hold at large u. The next term in a_D is constant beside the logarithm; the value found here is 3 ln 2 − 2.
  • a_D ≈ c₀(u − 1) and a ≈ a₀ + (i/π) a_D ln a_D hold near u = 1, with c₀ = i/2 and a₀ = 4/π from Section 6.
  • θ_eff ≈ −4 arg a holds at large u.

Devices of this page, not in the paper

  • The integrals of Section 6 are evaluated by Gauss–Legendre quadrature at u = 2i and carried to other points and round closed paths by the equation (1 − u²) P″ = P/4, which both integrals satisfy. Panel L checks the equation against the integrals.
  • The curve Im(a_D/a) = 0 is found numerically, one point on each ray from u = 0. The paper states the condition and does not draw the curve.
  • The base point decides which of two conjugate forms the matrix at u = −1 takes. From the lower half-plane it is the paper’s M₋₁ = [−1, 2 ; −2, 3] and the massless dyon has charges (1, −1); from the upper half-plane it is [3, 2 ; −2, −1] and the charges are (1, 1).

Not shown

  • The N = 2 multiplets and the low-energy Lagrangian in superspace (Section 2).
  • The coupling to gravity (Section 3.3).
  • The argument that the three singular points and these monodromies are the only consistent choice, and the uniqueness of the solution (Sections 5.2 and 6).
  • Matter hypermultiplets, which are the subject of the authors’ second paper.

Manual

Controls

  • Re u and Im u move the probe, the vacuum at which the periods, the coupling and the masses are computed.
  • Radius about u = ±1 and radius about both set the closed paths of panels F, G and H. The matrices do not depend on the radii.
  • n_m and n_e choose the state of panels C and F; mass m is the perturbation of panel J.
  • Pause and speed run the animations. Film moves through the eyes in order.
  • Clean view hides the text for recording. Readout check recomputes every eye at fixed settings and lists each value beside the paper’s.
  • Drag a view to turn it; click it, then scroll to zoom; double-click to reset.

Reading a panel

  • The first chip gives the section of the paper. The grade chips say what kind of statement it is.
  • Gold is a; cyan is a_D. Rose marks u = 1 and the monopole; cyan marks u = −1 and the dyon. White is the probe.
  • In the plane of u the real axis runs left to right and the imaginary axis runs away from the viewer.
  • Transport agrees with direct quadrature to about eight figures; matrices of integers are found to better than 10⁻⁹.
  • The eyes explore the paper; they do not grade it.

Guide

  • I. The low-energy theory of one photon multiplet has a metric Im τ |da|². Duality acts on (a_D, a) and leaves the metric unchanged.
  • II. The mass of every BPS state is √2 |a n_e + a_D n_m|. A state can decay only where a_D/a is real.
  • III. The functions a and a_D are not single valued on the u-plane. There are three singular points; at two of them a monopole or a dyon is massless; the monodromies satisfy M₁ M₋₁ = M∞. A mass term makes the monopoles condense.
  • IV. The pair (a_D, a) is the pair of periods of a differential on the curve y² = (x² − 1)(x − u).
